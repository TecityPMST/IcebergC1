Key judgments

Bottom line up front
KJ-1

Bifurcation is manufactured by policy, and in 2025–26 it was institutionalized.

What began as executive-discretion tariffs is now embedded in statute and plan: the FY2026 NDAA codified outbound-investment screening (COINS Act) and the BIOSECURE Act Cleary Gottlieb · Jan 26, de minimis repeal is law from July 2027 Fed. Register · Jun 26, and China's 15th Five-Year Plan (adopted March 2026) makes chips a "pillar industry," pledges "extraordinary measures" for chokepoints and references AGI for the first time Trivium China · Mar 26. Summits modulate the pace; the machinery ratchets regardless of them.

KJ-2

The centre of gravity has moved from tariffs to non-tariff separation.

The tariff wedge collapsed from a 127.2% peak (May 2025) to roughly 30% trade-weighted after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026 PIIE · Nov 25; GTA via China Briefing · Jul 26 — yet 2026 has seen the fastest-ever accumulation of entity listings, procurement bans, export-licence slow-walks and data/certification probes on both sides. Trade decoupling is shallow and rerouted; technology, defence-adjacent and materials decoupling is deep and compounding.

KJ-3

Technology is the deepest front — and in 2026 the dynamic inverted.

Washington now sells (H200 licensed at a 25% government revenue share, January 2026 BIS · Jan 26) but Beijing refuses to buy: state data centres are closed to foreign accelerators and the NDRC's reported ¥2tn compute build mandates ≥80% domestic technology TechTimes/NDRC · Jun 26. Nvidia's China data-centre revenue went to zero in the April 2026 quarter Nvidia via Kiplinger · May 26. Chinese industrial policy is now as large a driver of the split as US export controls — a structural change from 2018–2025.

KJ-4

Each side holds one chokepoint set, and both spent 2026 fortifying it.

China: 91% of rare-earth refining, ~90% of magnets, battery chemistry and a licensing regime it now enforces with criminal prosecutions and whistleblower rewards S&P Global · May 26; Morgan Lewis · Jul 26. The US: leading-edge compute, aircraft, and the financial system. In June 2026 Beijing struck directly at the subsidised US rare-earth rebuild (export bans on MP Materials and USA Rare Earth) Bloomberg · Jun 26 — evidence the fight is now about each other's escape routes, not just each other's markets.

KJ-5

Financial separation is advanced at the edges, untriggered at the core.

Primary markets have effectively split — Chinese issuers raised $12m in the US in H1 2026 against ~US$27bn in Hong Kong HKEX/Dealogic via Intl Finance · Aug 26; two-way FDI is dead; the PBOC is 21 months into a gold-buying streak while its Treasury holdings sit at an 18-year low Bloomberg · Aug 26; CNBC · May 26. But the core holds: the dollar is still 57% of reserves and 81% of trade finance IMF COFER · Jul 26; SWIFT · Jul 26, and ~$880bn of Chinese ADRs remain listed in New York — the largest untriggered fault line in the relationship HSBC via KrAsia · Apr 25.

KJ-6

Political calendars, not economics, set the escalation rhythm.

A reported Xi state visit to Washington on 24 September comes five weeks before the 3 November midterms; the entire truce architecture then unwinds in stages between 10 and 27 November 2026 unless renewed Axios · Jul 26; Pillsbury · Nov 25. Beijing's calendar runs to the 21st Party Congress in autumn 2027. Expect deliverables-and-quiet into November, and renewed freedom to escalate on both sides after it. Update 22 Sep: the visit is now set for 24 September — Xi's first US state visit since September 2015 — and the 10 November cliff is a triple expiry: the BIS affiliates-rule suspension, China's rare-earth pause, and the 178-item Section 301 exclusion list all lapse together Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26; J.P. Morgan · 18 Sep 26. See §A. Update 25 Sep: the summit extended the truce by only two months, to 10 January 2027 — past the midterms, APEC Shenzhen and the Miami G20 — with re-dating of the affiliates rule and exclusions not yet documented Fox News · 23 Sep 26; Bloomberg via The Edge · 24 Sep 26. See §B.

KJ-7

Base case: managed rivalry punctuated by rupture scares — with Taiwan as the tail.

Xi told Trump in Beijing that mishandling Taiwan would put the relationship "in great jeopardy," risking "clashes and even conflicts" Time · May 26; Trump has called Taiwan arms "a very good negotiating chip" and froze a $14bn package GTI · May 26. Strategic ambiguity is being personalised and transactionalised — stabilising in the short run, corrosive to deterrence over time. Scenario weights and tripwires in §7.

§A

Summit addendum — Washington, 24 September

Added 22 Sep 2026 · sell-side previews

Xi Jinping arrives in Washington on 24 September for his first US state visit since 2015, five weeks before the midterms and seven weeks before the truce architecture expires. Two sell-side previews now in the C1 folder — J.P. Morgan (18 Sep) and Morgan Stanley (17 Sep) — converge on the same shape of outcome the August study anticipated: an extension of managed rivalry, not a settlement. This addendum records what has changed since the 13 August data cut, what the Street expects, and how it moves our scenario weights.

A.1 What has changed since 13 August

  • The summit is confirmed and staffed. Xi is expected 23–25 September (JPM) / on 24 September (MS) — the first Chinese state visit to the US since September 2015. Bessent testified on 15 September that he would meet Vice Premier He Lifeng on 19–20 September to pre-clear the package, continuing a working channel that has run through Davos, Paris, London, Seoul and Geneva; Iran sanctions sit on that agenda alongside trade, rare earths, agriculture and AI J.P. Morgan · 18 Sep 26; Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26.
  • The sanctions frontier moved toward the financial core. On 24 August the Treasury launched "Operation Economic Outcast" — described by Bessent as an "Economic D-Day" — a secondary-sanctions regime against Iran's enablers with an explicit escalation ladder; Bessent declined to exempt China and later warned that Chinese banks intermediating Iranian oil revenue would be in scope. Enforcement against Iran-linked networks has already reached Chinese and Hong Kong institutions Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26; J.P. Morgan · 18 Sep 26. This is the escalation lever the August study said Washington had not yet pulled (§4.4): the ladder is now published, even if the bank-level rung remains unclimbed.
  • Grid and power equipment entered the control perimeter. Executive Order 14420 (26 August) adds grid/power equipment as a fifth "swing" sector alongside semiconductors, internet/data centres, healthcare and materials, with a 120-day DOE implementation window running to roughly late December 2026 Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26.
  • FCC controls are going category-wide. Restrictions have moved from named Chinese companies toward component-level controls on drones, routers, robotics and power inverters, and the FCC is reportedly considering restrictions on Chinese optical transceivers used in AI data centres Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26 — the ~70%-share dependency the August study flagged in §6 as running into the US AI build-out.
  • The Middle East re-escalated. After a month of relative calm, US–Iran fighting resumed in early September; the Houthis seized Mocha on 10 September and advanced to Perim Island on 11 September. Strait of Hormuz flows, 21.2m b/d in 2025, averaged 4.9m b/d in 2Q26; Bab el-Mandeb flows rose from 5.6m to 8.1m b/d as cargoes diverted. Brent reached $130.80 on 15 September; US retail gasoline $4.37 and diesel $6.31 per gallon J.P. Morgan · 18 Sep 26. J.P. Morgan reads Iran and the waterways as the summit's clearest geopolitical upside — China's access to Tehran converted into ceasefire support and pressure to keep routes open, potentially in exchange for narrower US secondary-sanctions spillovers; Morgan Stanley reads the same file as the clearest tail risk should Washington target non-dollar transactions J.P. Morgan · 18 Sep 26; Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26.
  • Beijing's pre-summit staging emphasised the alternative lattice. Xi attended the SCO summit in Bishkek (1 September) and the BRICS summit in New Delhi (12–13 September), reinforcing multilateralism, sanctions restraint and supply-chain resilience themes; he had made a state visit to Pyongyang on 8–9 June J.P. Morgan · 18 Sep 26.
  • AI has its own track. A first-ever official US–China AI safety dialogue, led by Bessent, was being arranged around mid-September; Chinese officials have described the AI talks as a major summit deliverable. Washington's asks: cooperation on monitoring AI-directed cyber-attacks, lab self-regulation and threat-sharing, and action on distillation of proprietary US models — with a "middle path" on restrictions that leaves below-frontier models alone while controlling frontier capabilities Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26.

A.4 Morgan Stanley's market scenario grid

For calibration against our own S1–S3 framework (§7), Morgan Stanley's China equity strategists map four summit outcomes: A — truce extended, broader deal (bull; MSCI China ~+5%, echoing the +6.5% two-week move after Geneva in May 2025 but smaller given a more stabilised starting point); B — truce extended, limited/symbolic (base case; muted index impact, "good enough" for low expectations; selective TMT/AI-datacentre relief); C — summit yields little / post-summit escalation (mostly sideways; localisation plays — grid equipment, WFE, EDA, rare earths — benefit as policy uncertainty reinforces self-sufficiency); D — cancellation or active escalation (tail; no more than a ~10% index correction, buy-the-dip depending on framing; AI/datacentre capex, biopharma cross-border deals, brokerages and airlines most exposed). The balance of pre-summit signals skews to B, with AI-specific escalation risk running in parallel on its own track Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26. Independent of scenario, MS treats domestic AI infrastructure, cloud, governance/security tooling and compute-plus-energy beneficiaries as a "structural buy" — while noting China equities face macro weakness, regulatory concerns and liquidity headwinds that outweigh the summit near term (index targets cut 3 September) Morgan Stanley · 17 Sep 26.

Judgment — Claude assessment, 22 Sep 2026 · how this moves our framework

Thesis unchanged, weights nudged. Both previews describe, in sell-side language, exactly the ratchet-and-throttle structure of §1: the summit will suspend (rare earths, affiliates rule, exclusions) and purchase (aircraft, engines, oil, LNG), and will touch nothing structural — controls, screening, subsidies, data. J.P. Morgan's "strategic accommodation within managed decoupling" is our KJ-7 restated. We move the 6–24 month weights to S1 managed rivalry 60% (from 55%) · S2 re-escalation/snapback 25% (from 30%) · S3 rupture 15% (unchanged). Two offsetting adjustments drive this: the near-term snapback probability falls because both sides are reportedly treating an extension as near-certain and have two further leader meetings (Shenzhen, Miami) to repair a partial one; but the composition of the rupture tail shifts from a security shock toward a financial one, because Operation Economic Outcast has published the bank-level escalation ladder and the Iran war has re-escalated with oil above $130. A partial renewal — extension of the rare-earth pause and exclusions but not the affiliates rule, or a shorter tenor than one year — is now the single most likely "disappointment" outcome and would sit inside S1, not S2.

Six tells to read the summit by (in order of importance): (1) tenor and scope of the rare-earth/affiliates/exclusions rollover — one year and all three is clean S1; (2) whether an Iran "guardrails" understanding appears (evidence procedures, named-entity action) — that is the JPM upside and removes the bank-sanction tail for a quarter; (3) whether the Board of Trade publishes an actual ~$30bn product list with a timetable, or only a communiqué; (4) whether the AI dialogue is institutionalised (standing channel, incident notification) or left as a photo; (5) any Taiwan language shift or arms-package movement in either direction; (6) whether the FCC optical-transceiver restriction is paused, proceeds, or is traded — the first direct test of whether Washington will protect its own AI build-out's Chinese dependency. §B

Post-summit update — what the summit delivered

Added 25 Sep 2026 · data to 25 Sep a.m. SGT

The Washington summit produced the minimum viable extension: the Busan truce rolls from 10 November to 10 January 2027 — two months, the shortest extension of the cycle — with no published tariff list, no new purchases, no chip or Iran understanding, and no White House readout. The ratchet-and-throttle model held exactly; what changed is that the throttle is now being set in two-month increments.

B.1 What happened, 20–25 September

The economic package was pre-cleared on the Sunday and Wednesday before the summit rather than at it. Bessent and He Lifeng met for roughly twelve hours in New York on 20 September, where USTR Greer said the Board of Trade had been "made operational" and the US tabled an AI-incident "notification mechanism"; they met again for ~90 minutes at the World Bank on 23 September, after which Bessent announced on Fox that "the Busan agreement, the economic détente… scheduled to end on Nov. 10… is going to be extended until Jan. 10" — adding that Beijing had floated "a bigger deal" and that Washington was "fine with both" paths Fox News · 23 Sep 26; Reuters via Korea Times · 24 Sep 26; investingLive · 21 Sep 26. Bloomberg reported the US had floated three to six months against Beijing's preference for a lock-in through the end of Trump's term; Bessent said Chinese deliverables had been "not perfect" and the $17bn-a-year agriculture commitment was "running behind" Bloomberg via The Edge/Yahoo · 24 Sep 26; SBS/MOFCOM · 24 Sep 26. MOFCOM confirmed that the 23 September session included the "first talks on AI under the framework of the US-China economic and trade negotiations" — but neither MOFCOM nor the MFA publicly confirmed the 10 January date; the Chinese readout refers only to "a new joint arrangement" SBS · 24 Sep 26; MFA readout · 25 Sep 26.

The state visit itself (23–25 September; Trump greeted Xi planeside at Andrews, a courtesy last extended to Pope Francis in 2015) ran on ceremony: a South Lawn arrival with flyovers, a ~90-minute Oval Office meeting attended on the Chinese side by Cai Qi, Wang Yi and He Lifeng, and an East Room dinner whose head table seated Jensen Huang, Lisa Su, Cristiano Amon, Sam Altman, Sundar Pichai, Jamie Dimon, Elon Musk and Tim Cook — and no Chinese executives; fewer than 20 of ~130 guests were Chinese, all officials PBS/AP · 23 Sep 26; Korea JoongAng/Xinhua · 25 Sep 26; Seoul Economic Daily · 25 Sep 26; CBS live · 24 Sep 26. B.3 Why two months — and why it matters more than the number

Three readings converge. Leverage retention: Washington chose the shortest option on the table and said why — Chinese performance on rare earths and agriculture has been "not perfect"; CSIS's Scott Kennedy reads the tenor as a signal that the US is "yet to be satisfied with China's offer on rare earths," while keeping "pressure on Beijing" and giving Xi a reason to attend the Miami G20 Bloomberg via The Edge · 24 Sep 26. Sequencing: 10 January sits after the 3 November midterms, after APEC Shenzhen and after the G20 — the cliff has been moved out of election week and behind two more leader meetings, without conceding the year Beijing wanted PBS/AP · 23 Sep 26. Withheld validation on the Chinese side: Beijing declined to confirm the date publicly, brought no business delegation, and had Fudan's Wu Xinbo articulate the counter-leverage: "within two months the US cannot become independent of Chinese rare-earth supply. They'll come back and beg" Bloomberg via Yahoo · 24 Sep 26; Seoul Economic Daily · 25 Sep 26. Atlantic Council's Melanie Hart had framed the US intent before the summit as buying time "to unwind China's dominance over rare earth supply chains… Once that is unwound, the administration plans to revert to tough action" Atlantic Council · 21 Sep 26.

The regime change for investors is the frequency of decision points, not the level of tariffs. A one-year rollover would have made the truce an annual event; a two-month rollover makes it a rolling short-dated option, re-struck at each leader meeting. Wendy Cutler's verdict — "big deliverables are just no longer possible" — and Oxford Economics' "calmer, but not closer" describe the same equilibrium the August study called competitive system-building under a ceasefire Bloomberg via ZeroHedge · 24 Sep 26; Reuters via Investing.com · 23 Sep 26.

B.4 Markets: fade confirmed, with two complications

§1

The frame: why politics is the engine

Two systems · one collision

Markets integrated the United States and China for thirty years; states are now separating them. That inversion is the essential fact of this cycle, and it is why the split is best analysed through politics and policymaking rather than through cost curves.

The two systems are structurally different in ways that make rivalry self-reinforcing. China is a Leninist party-state running state-directed capitalism: the Party sets five-year plans, allocates capital through policy banks and "Big Funds," and treats technology as the material base of national power — the Fourth Plenum communiqué that framed the 15th Five-Year Plan declared that "security is the prerequisite for development" and, for the first time in a plan-recommendation text, included a tasking to strengthen war-preparedness (备战) Jamestown Fdn · Oct 25; Brookings · Nov 25. The United States is an alliance-anchored market democracy whose China policy is produced by a genuinely bipartisan threat consensus in Congress (56 China-related hearings in 2025, up 47% year-on-year The Asia Group · Jan 26) layered under a transactional presidency that treats tariffs, export licences and even arms sales as negotiable instruments. Neither system can credibly reassure the other: Beijing cannot make binding commitments that outlast the Party's interest, and Washington cannot make commitments that outlast a news cycle, a court ruling, or an election.

The result is what scholars call weaponized interdependence: each side has converted its network positions into coercive tools. Washington weaponised semiconductors, the dollar clearing system and its investor base. Beijing weaponised rare earths, magnets, battery chemistry and access to its market — and in 2025–26 built a formal legal architecture to do so, mirroring the US toolkit instrument for instrument: an Unreliable Entity List (76 additions in 2025, versus 3 in 2024), a dual-use export-control list (82 additions in 2025), Anti-Foreign-Sanctions-Law designations, government-procurement bans, and — used against US measures for the first time in May 2026 — blocking rules that prohibit Chinese firms from complying with foreign sanctions WilmerHale · Jan 26; Asia Times · May 26.

The ratchet and the throttle

Two mechanisms now govern the relationship. The ratchet is the permanent bureaucratic-legislative machinery — entity lists, five-year plans, NDAA provisions, licensing regimes — which only ever tightens. The throttle is leader-level summitry, which can suspend but almost never repeal. The Busan truce (30 October 2025) is the emblem: China did not abolish its October 2025 rare-earth regime, it suspended it for exactly one year; the US did not abolish the 50% entity-list affiliates rule, it suspended it for the same year Pillsbury · Nov 25; Squire Patton Boggs · Nov 25. Both snap back on 10 November 2026 by default. Exhibit 1 shows the resulting rhythm: escalation, summit, suspension, drift, counter-listing, escalation.

Three features of this cycle matter for what follows. First, each list begets a counter-list: the Pentagon's June 1260H additions produced China's June 22 export bans within a fortnight; July's FCC and forced-labour actions produced the August 5 package within a week — Beijing explicitly called its response "restrained overall," language that advertises withheld escalation capacity SCMP · Aug 26. Second, both sides now escalate between summits and de-escalate at them, which makes the summit calendar the single best predictor of near-term intensity. Third, suspensions are not settlements: the entire 2025 arsenal — China's extraterritorial 0.1% rare-earth rule, the US affiliates rule, port fees on both sides — sits intact behind a timer set to 10–27 November 2026 Pillsbury · Nov 25; Holland & Knight · Nov 25.

Judgment — Claude assessment

The correct mental model is not "trade war" but competitive system-building under a ceasefire. Both governments used the 2025–26 truce exactly as their planning documents said they would: to reduce dependence on the other side faster than the other side reduces dependence on them. The truce is real and both leaders want it renewed — but every quarter it holds, the underlying separation deepens, because the ratchet works during the ceasefire.

§2

Washington's machinery

Consensus below · transaction above

US China policy in 2026 is produced by three factions sharing one threat assessment, refereed by a president who prices everything — and, since February, disciplined by the courts.

2.1 The factional map

The dealmakers own the file. Treasury Secretary Bessent leads strategy and summitry with USTR Greer running trade; the pair have fronted every negotiating round from Geneva (May 2025) through the July 2026 video call preparing Xi's Washington visit, where Bessent's public line — "we expect Beijing to fully meet its commitments on rare earths and U.S. agricultural products" — captured the administration's purchase-led definition of success Axios · Jul 26. Commerce Secretary Lutnick plays enforcer on export controls; Navarro-style maximalism has been sidelined in 2026 reporting Drezner · Apr 26.

The tech accelerationists won the biggest fights of the year. Hours after Jensen Huang met Trump on 3 December 2025, the GAIN AI Act — which would have forced US chipmakers to prioritise domestic buyers — was stripped from the NDAA Tom's Hardware · Dec 25; Bloomberg Govt · Dec 25. Five weeks later Commerce licensed H200 sales to China at an unprecedented 25% revenue share to the US government (up from the H20's 15%), with case-by-case review, third-party testing and a cap keeping China at ≤50% of volumes sold to US customers BIS · Jan 26. Export denial has been converted into export monetisation — controls as a tollbooth and bargaining chip rather than a wall.

The congressional hawks legislate what the executive won't. The FY2026 NDAA (December 2025) codified the outbound-investment programme into statute (COINS Act — expanding coverage beyond semiconductors/AI/quantum to supercomputing and hypersonics) and enacted the BIOSECURE Act's federal procurement ban on Chinese biotech "companies of concern," with the OMB blacklist due 18 December 2026 Cleary Gottlieb · Jan 26; Sidley · Dec 25. The Chip Security Act (location-verification for exported AI chips) cleared House Foreign Affairs on 26 March 2026 with, notably, industry support House Select Cttee · Mar 26; NBC · Mar 26. The pattern: hawks build statutory floors under a transactional presidency; the presidency kills only what threatens live deals.

2.2 The legal rebuild: courts as a fourth actor

On 20 February 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in Learning Resources v. Trump that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs — the majority holding that "regulate" does not encompass the power to tax, under the major-questions doctrine SCOTUS 24-1287 · Feb 26; K&L Gates · Feb 26. Collections stopped at midnight on 24 February. The administration rebuilt within hours: a 10% global Section 122 balance-of-payments tariff bridged 24 February to its 150-day statutory expiry on 24 July, surviving its own adverse court ruling in May via a Federal Circuit stay; on 24 July it was replaced by Section 301 "forced-labour" tariffs on 60 economies — +12.5 percentage points on China, taking the core Section 301 stack to 37.5% Wiley · Feb 26; Skadden · May 26; Honigman · Jul 26.

The fiscal consequences are material: roughly $164.7bn had been collected under IEEPA with refund exposure estimated up to $175bn; in May 2026, refund outflows briefly drove net monthly customs receipts to approximately zero Penn Wharton · Feb & Jul 26. The deeper consequence is structural: tariffs moved from instant executive discretion to slower, court-tested statutory channels — harder to erect, and harder for any future administration to unwind. Two legal notes matter for positioning: the CIT refund process (Phase 2 opened 29 June) is under government appeal, and the new forced-labour 301 is already being litigated Holland & Knight · Jun 26; CNBC · Jul 26.

Judgment — Claude assessment

Washington's system now produces two China policies simultaneously: a statutory one that only tightens (Congress + courts + bureaucracy) and a discretionary one that oscillates with the deal cycle (the presidency). For markets this means headline volatility around summits is mostly noise; the binding constraint on any détente is that the statutory floor keeps rising beneath it. Watch the gap between the two — e.g. a friendly Xi visit in September against a BIOSECURE list drop in December — as the recurring source of mispriced "thaw" narratives.

§3

Beijing's machinery

Plan below · stabilization above

China's policy system is running two programmes at once: a diplomatic stabilization line aimed at extending the truce, and a planning apparatus that assumes the truce ultimately fails.

3.1 The plan is the strategy

The 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), adopted at the March 2026 Two Sessions, is the clearest statement of intent available. It elevates semiconductors, aerospace and biotech to "pillar industries," pledges "extraordinary measures" (超常规措施) for chokepoint breakthroughs, targets R&D spending growth of 7% per year and a digital economy at 12.5% of GDP, embeds "AI+" across the economy, and — a first for national planning language — references AGI explicitly Trivium China · Mar 26; China Briefing · Mar 26; WilmerHale · Mar 26. The growth target was cut to a 4.5–5% range with a 4%-of-GDP deficit — a fiscal posture built for endurance rather than stimulus heroics Al Jazeera · Mar 26. The plan operationalises the Fourth Plenum's framing ("security is the prerequisite for development"; war-preparedness tasking) into resource allocation Jamestown · Oct 25.

Execution is visible in the capital plumbing: Big Fund III has pivoted from fab construction to the remaining bottlenecks — advanced packaging, equipment, materials and AI-chip design TrendForce · Aug 26 — while the NDRC's reported ¥2tn (~$295bn) national computing build to 2028 carries a ≥80% domestic-technology mandate TechTimes · Jun 26. Ideologically, nothing has softened: "great changes unseen in a century," dual circulation and self-reliance (自立自强) remain the frame, with Wang Yi simultaneously running the smile track — "mutual respect, peaceful coexistence, win-win cooperation" — through 2026 pressers and the summit season FMPRC · Feb–Mar 26. Struggle and stabilization are not alternating phases; they are parallel tracks.

3.2 The mirror-image coercive state

Between 2024 and 2026 Beijing assembled a complete legal counterpart to the US sanctions state, and 2025 was the year it went from display to daily use: 76 Unreliable Entity List additions in 2025 versus 3 in 2024; 82 export-control list additions; Anti-Foreign-Sanctions-Law designations that for the first time named site-level US defence units; procurement bans on 46 US defence-linked companies; a first-ever foreign-trade national-security investigation (August 2026, targeting US-software office equipment); and the first formal invocation of the 2021 Blocking Rules against US sanctions (May 2026), ordering Chinese firms and banks not to comply with the US designation of Hengli Petrochemical over Iranian crude WilmerHale · Jan 26; Al Jazeera · Jun 26; fredgao/MOFCOM · Aug 26; Asia Times · May 26.

The rare-earth regime is the template for how Beijing wields asymmetric leverage (detail in §4.3): a permanent licensing layer (April 2025, never suspended) that throttles by queue rather than by ban; a suspended escalation layer (October 2025 — five more elements, equipment and technology controls, and an extraterritorial 0.1% de-minimis rule reaching foreign-made goods) held as snapback ammunition; general licences dispensed as rewards to compliant buyers (issued from December 2025, disproportionately to Europeans); and, new in 2026, criminal enforcement and a whistleblower mechanism that extends liability to logistics, brokerage and finance providers Pillsbury · Nov 25; SCMP · Dec 25; MERICS · 2026; Morgan Lewis · Jul 26. MERICS's verdict — delays function as leverage with "plausible deniability" — describes the entire model MERICS · 2026.

3.3 The purge paradox

Xi enters the pre-Congress year politically dominant and institutionally exposed. The October 2025 Plenum expelled ten full Central Committee members including CMC vice-chair He Weidong and political-work chief Miao Hua; January 2026 brought investigations of CMC first vice-chair Zhang Youxia and joint-staff chief Liu Zhenli — leaving five of the six uniformed officers Xi appointed to the 2022 CMC purged and the commission at its smallest in PRC history Brookings · Nov 25; NBC · Jan 26; CSIS · Feb 26. The Pentagon's December 2025 China Military Power Report notes "short-term disruptions in operational effectiveness" — while confirming the strategic build continues: warheads in the low 600s heading past 1,000 by 2030, 100+ new ICBM silos loaded, the Fujian carrier commissioned DoD CMPR via FAS · Dec 25; USNI · Nov 25. Purges have not softened external policy; if anything they concentrate decision authority further in one man ahead of the 21st Party Congress (autumn 2027), where Xi is expected to refill the gutted CC/CMC on his own terms Brookings · Nov 25; Sinocism · Oct 25.

Judgment — Claude assessment

Beijing is the more patient actor in this cycle because its political system prices policy over five-year horizons while Washington's prices it over election cycles — but it is not the more secure one. The purge tempo, the growth-target cut and the record export dependence on non-US markets all signal a leadership optimising for regime resilience under protracted confrontation, not for near-term victory. The practical implication: Chinese concessions will be purchase-shaped (soybeans, Boeing) and never structure-shaped (subsidies, controls, data) — the 15th FYP forecloses structural concessions by design.

Judgment — Claude assessment

The 2026 inversion is strategically decisive: once both capitals prefer separation in compute, the tech split becomes self-executing and summit-proof — there is no longer a bargain in which chips reintegrate the two systems, only a price at which each tolerates the other's build-out. The remaining live questions are (1) enforcement — whether smuggled compute keeps supplying ~⅓ of China's stack, and (2) memory — whether CXMT breaks the HBM ceiling on schedule. On current evidence the model-capability gap is a poor bifurcation indicator (it compresses); the compute-stock and toolchain gaps are the real ones (they persist).

§4.3

Front: critical minerals & chokepoints

Depth: China's chokehold · US rebuild under fire

Rare earths are the model of Chinese economic statecraft: a permanent licensing throttle, a suspended escalation layer held as snapback ammunition, and — new in 2026 — direct strikes on America's escape route. Deepening · asymmetric

4.3.1 The two-layer regime and the November clock

Layer one, in force: the April 2025 dual-use licensing regime over seven medium/heavy rare earths (Sm, Gd, Tb, Dy, Lu, Sc, Y) and magnets — never suspended, operated on a ~45-working-day clock that in practice runs months, with a two-track pattern: European civilian users cleared "relatively quickly," US and defence-linked applicants delayed or denied S&P Global · May 26; MERICS · 2026; Reuters · 2025. Layer two, suspended to 10 November 2026: the October 2025 expansion — five more elements, controls on rare-earth equipment, materials and technology, lithium-battery and graphite-anode controls, and the extraterritorial rule capturing any foreign-made item with ≥0.1% Chinese rare-earth content or made with Chinese RE technology CIRS · Nov 25; Pillsbury · Nov 25. The gallium/germanium/antimony export ban on the US is likewise only suspended, to 27 November 2026 CNBC · Nov 25.

The licensing throttle shows up unambiguously in flows. China's total 2025 rare-earth exports hit a decade high of 62,585t (+12.9%) — while US-bound shipments never recovered: yttrium deliveries to the US ran 17t in April–December 2025 versus 333t in the prior eight months, still ~20t/month in February 2026; November 2025 shipments to the EU rose 60% YoY while US-bound fell 11%; and as of the May summit, Dy/Tb/Y exports remained ~50% below pre-control levels Reuters · Jan 26; CSIS · Apr 26; Mining.com · May 26. At that summit the White House said China "will address US concerns" on yttrium, scandium, neodymium and indium; MOFCOM's readout did not mention rare earths at all — Reuters called the outcome a tacit acknowledgement that "China's export control regime is here to stay" White House · May 26; Reuters/WHBL · May 26.

4.3.2 June 22: the strike on the American rebuild

The US counter-build is real and accelerating: ~$7.3bn of federal commitments across five agencies; the DoD–MP Materials structure ($400m equity, $110/kg NdPr price floor, the 10X magnet plant targeting ~2028); a $1.6bn package for USA Rare Earth's Round Top; Lynas achieving the first terbium separation outside China (June 2026); the US–Australia framework ($8.5bn pipeline, expanded by >$5bn in April 2026); a 49% US stake in a Saudi refinery; and a Section 232 critical-minerals proclamation (January 2026) reserving minimum-import-price authority CSIS · Apr 26; Investing.com · Aug 26; Aus. Trade Minister · Apr 26; Foley · Jan 26. §4.4

Front: finance & capital

Depth: edges split · core untriggered

Capital is bifurcating in a distinctive pattern: primary markets and direct investment have already separated, official reserves are quietly diverging, payment plumbing is being duplicated — while the two largest linkages, the dollar system and $880bn of US-listed Chinese equity, remain intact and hostage-like. Deepening · sequenced last

4.4.1 Walls on both gates

Outbound: the Treasury's investment-screening programme (effective January 2025, covering semiconductors, AI and quantum) was codified by the COINS Act in the FY2026 NDAA and is expanding to supercomputing and hypersonics, with new regulations due within 450 days — statute now, not executive order Cleary Gottlieb · Jan 26. Inbound: the America First Investment Policy memo set CFIUS against Chinese capital; a July 2025 federal plan targets Chinese farmland ownership including clawbacks; and state pension systems (Indiana, Florida, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Texas directive) are executing China divestment mandates Crowell · Feb 25; WaPo · Jul 25; Ropes & Gray · 2025. The measured result: Chinese FDI into the US fell below $3bn announced in 2025 — the lowest since the mid-2010s, with ~80% of Chinese US battery investment cancelled or suspended — while China's own inbound FDI fell 9.5% in 2025 after 2024's record net-outflow year Rhodium · Apr 26; China Briefing/MOFCOM · Jan 26; Bloomberg · Feb 25.

4.4.2 The listings split and the untriggered fault line

Primary equity issuance has bifurcated almost completely. H1 2026: one Chinese listing in the US raising $12m, against 85 Hong Kong IPOs raising HK$209.9bn (~US$27bn) — with HKEX's seven-month tally at HK$328bn, +154% YoY, a 443-deal pipeline, and China's AI champions (Moonshot, MiniMax, Biren) pre-committing to A+H structures that bypass New York entirely Intl Finance/HKEX · Aug 26; KPMG · Jun 26; Seoul Economic Daily · Jul 26. The sanctions perimeter is tightening by increments: five Chinese refineries designated over Iranian crude between March 2025 and April 2026, culminating in Hengli Petrochemical — the first major-scale target — followed by an OFAC alert warning global banks over servicing China's teapot refiners (noting China buys ~90% of Iran's oil exports) Asia Times · May 26; US Treasury · Apr 26. §4.5

Front: security & alliances

Depth: hardening beneath the summitry

The security domain shows the widest gap between atmospherics and structure: 2026 has delivered the calmest cross-Strait tempo in years and the warmest leader diplomacy of the decade — atop the fastest allied rearmament since the Cold War and a Chinese nuclear build-out running exactly to schedule. Structurally deep

4.5.1 Taiwan: ambiguity, personalised

The May summit put the Taiwan question at the centre of leader politics. Xi warned that mishandling Taiwan would put the relationship "in great jeopardy," risking "clashes and even conflicts"; Rubio insisted US policy was "unchanged" Time · May 26; CFR · May 26. Trump's follow-on was the departure: Taiwan arms are "a very good negotiating chip," and the $14bn January 2026 package — the largest ever assembled — was held "in abeyance," with the acting Navy secretary telling the Senate the pause also preserved munitions after the Iran war (US–Iran ceasefire 8 April 2026) GTI · May 26; Al Jazeera · May 26; WaPo · May 26. Taipei says it was never notified; eight bipartisan senators protested; a December 2025 package (>$10bn: HIMARS, ATACMS, howitzers) had already been approved, and Washington quietly publicised the first combined US–Taiwan coast-guard patrol in July 2026 Newsweek · May 26; NPR · Dec 25; AEI · Aug 26. Policy is thus running both directions at once — arms as leverage and deepening operational cooperation.

On the island: the 2025 mass-recall campaign failed completely, locking in opposition control of the legislature through 2028 and repeatedly blocking Lai's NT$1.25tn (~$40bn) eight-year special defence budget — yet the trend spending line is unambiguous: 3.32% of GDP in 2026, a record NT$1.1tn (+16%) planned for 2027, and a stated path to 5% by 2030 CFR/Brookings · 2025; GTI · Mar 26; Bloomberg · Aug 26. 4.5.2 The alliance lattice hardens as America re-prices it

Around China's periphery, 2025–26 produced the fastest allied military build-up in decades — much of it driven by China's own coercion. Judgment — Claude assessment

The 2026 calm is rentable, not ownable: it rests on two leaders' simultaneous need for quiet (midterms; Party Congress) and on transactional handling of Taiwan that both reassures and unsettles Taipei. The structural indicators — nuclear trajectory, exercise-to-blockade morphing, allied budgets, munitions strain — all point to a harder 2027–28 window regardless of summit outcomes. The single most under-priced risk in the set: a Scarborough-type incident under a US treaty commitment, which unlike Taiwan carries an explicit defence obligation and a demonstrated 2026 escalation pattern.

§4.6

Front: institutions & the Global South

Depth: parallel systems rising

The organising layer of the old order is splitting into a US-anchored lattice of bilateral deals and a China-anchored lattice of plurilateral institutions — with most of the world deliberately enrolled in both. Partial · accelerating

The old commons is decaying. Hedging, not bloc discipline, is the global default — which is precisely why both powers now compete on the quality of what they can offer the middle, and why the tariff-differential compression described in §4.1 matters strategically as well as commercially.

§6

Where bifurcation is not happening

The couplings that persist

A rigorous bifurcation thesis must account for the evidence against it. Six couplings are holding — some by economics, some by deliberate mutual hostage-taking.

  • The world still buys China — including America, indirectly. China's export machine set records straight through the trade war ($1.2tn surplus 2025; +17.6% H1 2026), and rerouting means US consumption still embeds Chinese content via Vietnam, Mexico and ASEAN — now with tariff differentials so compressed (12.5% China add-on vs 10–12.5% on 60 others) that PIIE's Bown argues the structure "could drive companies back to China" AP · Jan 26; SCMP · Jul 26; PIIE · Aug 26.
  • The US AI build-out runs on Chinese components. Chinese suppliers hold roughly 70% of the global optical-module market feeding US data centres — a dependency running into the flagship US strategic project, largely undiscussed in tariff schedules fredgao · Aug 26.
  • Corporate America has not left. AmCham China 2026: 57% of members plan to increase China investment; 71% are not considering relocation; US pharma signed a record $137.7bn of Chinese biotech in-licensing in 2025 AmCham/Global Times · Jan 26; PharmaSource · 2026.
  • The dollar core and the ADR stock are intact. USD: 57% of reserves, 81% of trade finance, record foreign UST demand; ~$880bn of Chinese ADRs still trade in New York; the PCAOB inspection regime still functions; Treasury's own outbound rules preserved the public-securities carve-out IMF/SWIFT · Jul 26; KrAsia · Apr 25; Cleary · Dec 25.
  • Hong Kong is functioning as the sanctioned connector. The listings boom is Western-bank intermediated; HKD stablecoins got licences while RMB ones were banned; both systems find HK useful precisely because it straddles KPMG · Jun 26; Forbes · Aug 26.
  • Both leaders still want the truce. Beijing labels its own retaliation "restrained"; Washington times blacklists between summits; purchases, Boeing orders and chip licences are all structured as renewable leases on stability SCMP · Aug 26.
Judgment — Claude assessment

These couplings are real but asymmetrically fragile: every one of them survives at policy sufferance — a delisting bill, a snapback, a blocked strait — while none of the separations built since 2018 would reverse even under a warm détente. Bifurcation is best understood as a ratchet operating behind a curtain of interdependence: the couplings are what remains, not what is being rebuilt.

Investment implications

Read-throughs · all judgment
Judgment — positioning read-throughs (Claude assessment; not advice, size to house risk limits)

Framing. Bifurcation is a capex engine and a repricing regime, not a single trade. The ratchet (statutes, plans) is investable on multi-year horizons; the throttle (summits) generates event risk around the calendar in §7. The structural error to avoid is pricing détente off summit atmospherics while the statutory floor rises — 2026 has already punished that twice.

1 · Two-stack compute. The US stack's China revenue is now ~zero and Gulf/allied demand is the growth offset (UAE A:5; ~$600bn hyperscaler capex) — China risk to US semis has become policy-option risk (snapback, Chip Security Act compliance costs) rather than demand risk; the 26 Aug Nvidia print is the marker. The China stack (Cambricon +108%, Huawei chain, SMIC/CXMT ecosystem, Big Fund III packaging/equipment targets) carries policy-guaranteed demand via the ¥2tn grid and 80% mandate — crowded and expensively valued on A/H markets, but the earnings are now real; access is via the HK/A pipeline (Moonshot, MiniMax, Biren listings), not ADRs. HBM (CXMT execution) is the single best fundamental tell inside the stack.

2 · Critical minerals ex-China. Western producers now enjoy a policy floor (DoD's $110/kg NdPr floor vs spot ~$133; price-floor language in the Australia framework) plus a validation signal — Beijing banning exports to MP/USA Rare Earth marks them as strategically consequential while barely touching their operations (MP: China dependencies "already largely eliminated"). Magnet scarcity persists to 2028+ on IEA math. Key risks: snapback-driven price spikes cut both ways (input costs for magnet-makers), and the trade is consensus after July 2025.

3 · Allied rearmament. Japan at 2% early with Tomahawk integration, Taiwan +16% in 2027 with T-Dome, AUKUS endorsed, US nuclear +15% and Golden Dome $17.9bn, munitions restocking after the Iran war — a decade-visible order book across missiles/munitions, shipbuilding (note the dormant-but-revivable US port-fee/shipbuilding programme), C4ISR and drones, with Japanese and Korean primes the under-owned route relative to US names.

4 · De-dollarisation hedges. The gold thesis is central-bank structural (21-month PBOC streak; record Q2 CB buying; freeze-risk hedging) — but entry matters after a −7% YTD consolidation from the $5,405 January high; WGC's base case is range-bound H2. RMB-lane assets (panda bonds, CGBs — foreign inflows resumed May) are carry-with-optionality on the parallel-plumbing build. This is a hedge book, not a dollar-collapse bet: COFER at 57% says the core holds.

5 · The compression reversal. The narrowed China-vs-alternatives tariff wedge undermines pure tariff-arbitrage relocation (Vietnam/Mexico assembly) while leaving genuine capability relocation (India electronics ecosystems, Mexico integration) intact — favour the latter, and watch transshipment/rules-of-origin enforcement as the swing variable. Within China exposure, the barbell is self-reliance beneficiaries (semis, industrial software, defence) plus exporters levered to ASEAN/Global South demand — the US-revenue-exposed names carry the delisting/1260H tail.

Event-risk book. Hedge the 10–27 Nov 2026 window explicitly (RE-exposed industrials, US semis with China optionality, CNH, TAIEX beta); treat SEC/1260H trading-suspension headlines as the ADR regime-change signal; treat any US designation of a major Chinese bank as the financial-escalation regime-change signal.
